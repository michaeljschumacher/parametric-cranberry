# Parametric Cranberry Frost Insurance

## Problem
Wisconsin cranberry growers face significant frost risk during bud development each spring. Current Multi-Peril Crop Insurance (MPCI) provides yield-based coverage with frost as a covered cause of loss, but policies are poorly constructed for frost specifically. Growers are exposed to losses in developmental stages of the growth cycle, yet indemnities are paid based on yield shortfall at harvest. A parametric insurance product offers immediate financial relief to cover farm costs incurred due to frost losses.

## Approach
Parametric frost coverage products exist for specialty crops such as cranberries, but fall short in mapping an effective temperature trigger to crops whose vulnerability to frost changes based on stages of plant development. This project introduces the concept of stage-dependent temperature triggers, with a ladder of triggers corresponding to the vulnerability at specific stages of plant development, indexed by calendar-based stage assignments. 

## Validation
The notebook initially explores county-level weather data via interpolation from nearby weather stations and loss data from USDA RMA MPCI Cranberry policies across five Wisconsin counties (Wood, Jackson, Adams, Juneau, Portage) from 2006-2024. The stage-dependent trigger is first validated against all-peril loss ratios, then against a bootstrapped loss ratio restricting to frost, freeze, and cold-wet-weather indemnities. Findings from both methods provide signal of a pronounced loss in 2021. All five counties experienced elevated all-peril and bootstrapped loss ratios, with the temperature degree trigger firing in four of the five counties and falling within 2 degrees Fahrenheit of firing in Wood County. A second year with elevated frost-related losses, 2012, was missed by the temperature trigger, likely due to a unusually warm early spring. These findings in both years are supported by public news sources, detailing the exact mechanism which causes frost losses this product aims to cover: warm early spring which accelerates plant development, followed by a severe frost relative to development stage. 

## Limitations
After initial exploration, this project has several known limitations. Calendar-based stage assignments assume that plant growth is uniform across years and is effectively modeled by date. This framework misses years where phenology is shifted, including the 2012 event noted above, so exploration of alternative development indexes are warranted. County-level calibration of temperature thresholds may be necessary to address the difference in false-positive rates across counties. Cold Wet Weather is a cause of loss which partially maps to classical frost and freeze losses with distinct trigger mechanisms, and its place within the scope of a parametric frost product remains an open question. Data on frost and freeze indemnities is quite sparse prior to 2014, with determination not made on this being a genuine trend or a change in the granularity of RMA data reporting. 

## Next Steps
Topics of future analysis are proposed to address the limitations noted above. The highest priority is development of growing-degree-day (GDD) based stage modeling, where development stage is assigned based on accumulation of days with conditions conducive to plant growth. This update over calendar-based stage assignments will more effectively model the key mechanism of frost loss as seen in 2021. Once an effective model for development-stage is tested against historical loss data, stochastic temperature modeling using mean-reverting processes will provide the foundation for forward-looking pricing.
